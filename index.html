The real problem

Your bill isn't about how much power you use. It's how much you use at once.

On Time-of-Use with Demand plans, your utility finds the single highest 15-minute spike in your month and charges you for it. When your AC, water heater, dryer and EV charger all fire in the same quarter hour, that one spike inflates the entire bill.

Utilities across Arizona, Georgia, the Carolinas and Virginia are moving more homes onto these plans and raising rates to cover grid strain. Without active demand control, bills go up, and up, and up.

Common questions

Straight answers before you call.

How much can I actually save?

Most customers save between 30% and 60% on their electric bills, depending on their utility's demand rates and the demand limit they set. Managing non-critical loads lets you take advantage of lower rates and get the most out of every kilowatt-hour you pay for.

How fast does it pay for itself?

The majority of homeowners see a full return on investment within 1 to 5 years. After that, every dollar saved goes straight to your bottom line, year after year. Financing options are available.

Will I notice it running? Does my AC get turned off?

Think of it like cruise control for your home. The system smooths out the peaks and valleys in your usage by briefly staggering appliances, not shutting them off. Your home stays at the temperature you set and hot water is always ready.

I already have solar. Does this still help?

Yes. Solar lowers the energy portion of your bill, but it does not control the demand portion, especially after sunset when peak windows hit. The DAC coordinates your loads so solar and battery storage work smarter and your demand charge stays low.
